visual-qa

Measure your pages the way people see them, then fix what fails

Use it when A page looks cramped, empty, too small or broken on a phone, or you are about to call a page done and want proof it holds up on real screens.

1What it measures

01

Three Screen Sizes

Every page is captured on two laptops and a phone

Each page is opened at 1440×900, 1280×800 and 390×844, the way a person would see it. A layout that only works on your own monitor gets caught.

1440×900
1280×800
390×844
02

No Sideways Scroll

Flags any page that scrolls sideways

A page that is wider than the screen fails, with the number of extra pixels. Wide tables are expected to scroll inside their own box instead of dragging the whole page.

390 · /pricing
Sideways scroll212 pxFAIL
Table scrolls in its box0 pxPASS
03

Text You Can Read

Flags text smaller than 16 px, even inside charts

Every piece of visible text is measured, including chart labels at the size they actually render. The floor is 16 px by default, and you can raise it for an older audience.

Body text18 pxPASS
Chart labels11 pxFAIL
Footnotes13 pxFAIL
floor: 16 px · adjustable
04

Big Enough to Tap

Buttons and links under 44 px get flagged

Every button, link and form field is checked for a 44 px tap area both ways. Links inside a sentence are left alone, since nobody expects those to be big.

EditDelete
32 × 26 · too small
EditDelete
48 × 44 · OK
05

Nothing Cut Off

Catches text cut short while there's room beside it

Titles that end in an ellipsis even though there is plenty of space next to them get flagged. You see the full name unless the screen truly has no room for it.

Wildflower Ho…180 px free
Cut off with room1FAIL
06

No Broken Words

Catches words split in the middle across two lines

A name that breaks into "Wildflo" on one line and "wer" on the next gets flagged, along with the CSS fix. Long names with slashes get a clean break point after the slash instead.

BeforeWildflo
wer Honeysplit word
AfterWildflower
Honeywhole words
07

How Much Fits

Counts how many items fit on the first screen

On lists, tables and stores, it counts what you see before scrolling and how much space the header takes first. Targets like six list lines on a laptop and three on a phone turn "feels empty" into a number.

Before · 1 item
After · 8 items
08

Uses the Screen

Desktop content should fill at least 70% of the width

A narrow column floating in a wide screen is flagged with how much of the width it uses. Content is expected to fill most of the screen, capped near 1200 px and centered.

Content width48%FAIL
target: at least 70%

2Real pages, real states

09

Every State, Not Just One

Full, empty, long text and missing values each get a look

Each page is captured in every state a person can reach: full of data, empty, with very long names, with missing values, and as each kind of user. You list the pages and states in a small pages file (an example is bundled). The empty state is where layouts usually break.

10

Open the Drawers

Sheets, dialogs and expanders are opened and measured

Anything that only appears after a click, like a slide-up sheet, a dialog or an expanding section, is opened the way a person would and measured as its own state.

11

Signed In for Real

Signs in through your real form, password never shown

Pages behind a login are reached by signing in through the actual sign-in form, using a small sign-in hook you write (an example is bundled). The hook reads the password from an untracked env file and never prints it, and any secret on screen is blanked before the screenshot.

email: dev@example.compassword: read from your env file
never printed secrets blanked before capture
12

Realistic Data, Put Back After

Tests with lifelike data, then restores what was there

Before capturing, the feature is filled with data shaped like real use: realistic counts, long names, blanks. The starting state is noted first and put back afterward.

Start · 3 itemsnoted
Seeded · 120 itemslong names, blanks
Restored · 3 itemsput back
13

Every Page of the Feature

Checks every page your change touches, not just one

Point it at a few pages, or give it none and it checks every page the current branch changes. You don't find the next broken page yourself after it said the first one was fine.

/proofs:visual-qa
/store /store/item /my/list /my/orders

3What you get

14

A Summary Table

One line per page and size, with what failed

A short table lists every page, state and screen size with its failures and counts. You see at a glance which page breaks where.

store  1440  none
store   390  small taps: 6
list   1280  small text: 4
list    390  sideways: 212 px
15

Screenshots at Every Size

A first-screen and a full-page picture of each

Every page, state and size gets two screenshots, what you see before scrolling and the whole page, named so they line up. They become the evidence for every finding.

store-full-1440-full.pngwhole page
store-full-390.pngphone
16

Looked At, Not Just Measured

Claude opens every screenshot itself

Numbers miss things like a label sitting on a bar, two items both called "Other" or a missing way back. Every screenshot is looked at directly, plus the in-between tablet widths where layouts switch.

390 · /store
label sits on the bar
also checked at 768 and 1024
17

A Ranked Audit

Problems listed by how much they hurt

The audit has a pass/fail table per page, then the problems ranked by how much they get in a person's way. Each one names the page, size, screenshot, number and a proposed fix.

1390 · buttons 32 px tall
21280 · 2 lines on first screen
31440 · footnotes at 13 px

Right Rules for the Page

Density targets for data pages, judgment for articles

Lists, tables, stores and dashboards get the item-count targets. Articles and landing pages keep the text, tap, scroll and cut-off checks, with density judged by eye.

4From problem to fix

19

Your Page as the Before

Each problem becomes a design review with a real screenshot

Every problem, or group of problems with one fix, becomes an item in a design review. The Before is the real screenshot, or the one you sent if you sent one.

Before · 390 · /store
1 item on first screen
20

Mockups That Prove It

Each proposed fix visibly meets its target

Mockups use your page's own fonts, colors and data, and are drawn to show the target being met: a laptop frame that really holds eight lines, a phone frame with 16 px text and 44 px buttons.

1280 × 800
8 lines visible 16 px text
21

The Number in the Reason

Every item states the failing number and the target

Each review item's reason gives the measured number next to the target it missed. You decide on facts, not on how a page feels.

Store listRecommended
Reason: 2 lines on the first screen at 1280; target is 6 or more.
22

Your Call Stays Yours

Choices that aren't layout fixes become questions

Anything that is really your decision, like whether to hide sold-out items, is asked as a question rather than quietly decided as part of a layout fix.

Question item
Hide sold-out items from the list?
Yes, hide themKeep showing
23

Before and After

Fixes are re-measured and shown side by side

After the approved fixes are built, the same pages are captured again with matching file names. You get Before and After screenshots side by side with the key numbers, before anything is merged.

Before
After
lines on screen   1 → 8

24

Locked In With a Test

Numbers worth keeping get pinned in a test

A number that matters, like how many rows fit or how many database queries a page makes, can be pinned in an automated test so a later change can't quietly undo the fix.

test "first screen holds 8 lines"  assert visible_lines >= 8
fails if it regresses
25

Where the Page Goes

A private link, a local file, or your Wi-Fi

Your project picks where review pages go: a private claude.ai link, an HTML file that opens in your browser, or that file served on your home Wi-Fi so a phone can open it. Nothing is ever uploaded or tunneled in the Wi-Fi mode, and the server stops when the review ends.
