01

Risky Claims, Checked First

A claims check is always the first section

Before your own feedback is touched, the whole page is read for claims that could get you in trouble, and the findings appear first. It runs every time, even when your feedback says nothing about claims.

02

What's Risky in Your Field?

Claude asks which claims are risky in your line of work

Risky claims depend on the business: health and wellness sites, product-safety claims, financial results and so on each have their own. Claude looks in your project notes, asks once if they don't say, and keeps the answer in the notes so it does not ask again. It checks for those claims on top of the general list.

What claims are risky in your field?
supplements: no cure or treatment claims
03

The Usual Suspects

Guarantees, cure claims, results without a caveat, risky headlines

The check always looks for guarantee language, claims to diagnose or cure, results or testimonials with no "results vary" note, and headlines that read as a claim on their own, since titles end up in tabs, search results and link previews.

guaranteed resultsRisky
Cures back painRisky
Results vary by personSafe
04

Missing Affiliate Disclosures

Pages with affiliate links but no disclosure get flagged

If a page recommends products through affiliate links without the disclosure the FTC requires, it is flagged, even when fixing it is outside this review.

Product picksNo disclosure
Affiliate links found, but no "we may earn a commission" note.
05

A Clean Check Still Shows

No findings is shown as a passed check, not a missing section

When nothing is found, the section still appears and says no concerns were found in this pass. You can see the check happened rather than wondering whether it was skipped.

1 · Claims checkPassed
No claims concerns found in this pass.

The Exact Words

Before and after show the real text, never a summary

The Original and Edit panes always hold the exact sentence a reader would see, written out in full even when the feedback only described the change. You decide on real copy.

See Exactly What Changed

Changed words are highlighted inside the sentence

Inside each pair, only the words that changed are marked, so a one-word fix in a long paragraph is easy to spot.

Looks Like Your Page

Uses your page's own type and before/after colors

The review page borrows the real page's fonts and colors, including any before/after colors your site already uses, and works in light and dark mode. The edits read the way they will on the site.

Claims check, then copy edits, then additions

Items are grouped as Claims check, Copy Edits, and Additions and Recommendations, always in that order, each item numbered.

An Honest Opinion

Every item has a reason and a recommendation

Each item says why the change is proposed and carries a Recommended or Not Recommended badge. Items Claude has doubts about are still shown, marked Not Recommended with the reason, so you can overrule it.

Keep, use, approve with a comment, or ask for another try

Each item has Keep Original, Use Edit, Approve with Comment and Request Revision. Every item starts undecided, and only one choice can be active.

Only What You Approved

Accepted edits are applied, with your comments worked in

Accepted edits go into the file as shown. Approved-with-comment edits are rewritten to follow your comment first. Kept, revise and undecided items leave the file untouched.

Every button is clicked before the page reaches you

Before each publish, the page's script is syntax-checked and the page is loaded and clicked through automatically. You don't get a page that looks fine but shows no items.

Just the Revisions

The next round brings back only what's still open

Items you sent back come back in a new round at the same link, with new proposals. Anything already decided stays settled and isn't shown again.

Nothing Undecided Gets Lost

Items you never got to are carried into the next round

An item you didn't decide on isn't dropped when the next round is built around your revisions. Every item without a final decision comes forward, so you always have a way to decide it.

Where the Page Goes
